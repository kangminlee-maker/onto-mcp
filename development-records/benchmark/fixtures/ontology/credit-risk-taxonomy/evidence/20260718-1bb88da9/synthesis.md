# Synthesize

## Consensus
- issue-001 (high): Multiple rating scales coexist without an ontology-owned conversion authority, so shared grade meaning can diverge between risk-engine and reporting consumers while still appearing compliant with the ontology.
  - root cause: Rating conversion is not modeled as a governed, versioned ontology concept, so grade meaning and continuity are delegated to external wiki or consumer implementations.
  - materiality: This is material because the ontology is meant to be the shared concept authority for risk engines and reporting systems. Rating grades are purpose-critical to that shared authority: if R1-R5, 1-10, and AAA-CCC conversions are left to local consumers or external wiki mappings, the same borrower or exposure can receive different classifications across engine output, reports, and historical comparisons.
  - action: Promote rating scales and mappings into governed, versioned ontology concepts. The fix should define authoritative mappings among the three scales, source authority, effective_from/effective_to or version fields, precedence between engine-derived and reporting-adjusted grades, and audit semantics for any allowed override, including reason, actor, timestamp/as_of, and audit status. This should be closed before the next stage because downstream reporting and engine behavior depend on stable shared grade meaning.
- issue-002 (high): Limit utilization is materially under-specified because the ontology defines a shared risk/reporting formula without making its calculation time, data freshness, FX basis, numerator, denominator, and CRM or external limit-source inputs authoritative parts of the contract.
  - root cause: Limit-utilization is modeled without versioned calculation inputs for data cutoff, calculation time, FX source, and external limit source authority.
  - materiality: This weakens the declared purpose of a shared risk-engine and reporting basis: the same utilization rule can be read as a month-end reporting snapshot, an intraday control value, or a system-local calculation depending on which exposure aggregate, FX table, and limit-source state a consumer uses. That can make utilization, breach pressure, and exposure reporting diverge across systems while appearing to use the same ontology concept.
  - action: Model ExposureAggregate and LimitUtilization as calculation outputs, not just stored values or prose-backed formulas. Add authoritative calculation_as_of, data_cutoff_at, calculation_version, fx_rate_source, fx_rate_date, and included_limit_sources; represent CRM or other external limit contributions as explicit components with source system and effective interval. This should be fixed before the next stage because later reporting, risk-engine, CRM, and FX extensions depend on the same utilization contract.
- issue-004 (high): Manual Exposure.risk_grade overrides are a material issue because the ontology permits reporting-side grade changes but does not model how a reported grade resolves from an engine-derived value versus an approved override, nor does it preserve the evidence needed to audit that override.
  - root cause: The ontology models adjusted risk grade as a bare Exposure attribute while placing the manual adjustment rule only in free-text classification_rules.
  - materiality: The ontology is meant to be a shared concept authority for risk engine and reporting classifications. When a core classification value can be manually changed without actor, time, reason, approval, source grade, adjusted grade, and effective/resolution evidence, downstream users cannot trust or reconstruct whether the reported grade came from the engine or from reporting judgment.
  - action: Add a bounded RiskGradeAdjustment or equivalent value-resolution concept before the next stage. It should connect the exposure to the source RiskRating or source grade, adjusted grade, actor, adjusted_at time, reason or market basis, approval reference/status, and effective period, and define that Exposure.risk_grade resolves to the latest effective approved adjustment when present or to the derived RiskRating mapping otherwise.
- issue-005 (high): Time-dependent ratings, limits, mappings, and exchange-rate inputs are modeled as current or undated values, so the ontology cannot reliably reconstruct the values that were valid for a historical report or risk-engine decision.
  - root cause: Temporal coverage is applied only selectively rather than as a concept dimension for all point-in-time risk inputs.
  - materiality: This weakens the declared purpose of shared risk-engine and reporting interpretation because historical and current credit-risk values require point-in-time authority. If ratings, approved limits, risk grades, mappings, and FX rates are not temporally qualified, two systems can replay the same reporting date or decision using different implicit values, making reports non-reproducible and calculations inconsistent.
  - action: Add temporal validity semantics consistently to all time-dependent risk inputs, using effective_from/effective_to, as_of, version, or equivalent history fields where appropriate. Also introduce an authoritative ExchangeRate or CurrencyConversionRate concept that records source system or table authority, currency pair, rate date or time, and rate value. This should be fixed before the next stage because downstream classification, utilization, reporting, and replay logic depend on these values having point-in-time authority.
- issue-006 (high): Values shared across the risk engine and reporting systems are not governed by a single ontology authority. Grade mappings, limit values, and exchange-rate sources are delegated to external or per-system sources, so consumers must reconcile them independently.
  - root cause: The ontology acknowledges cross-system coexistence but does not promote mapping, limit availability, or exchange-rate authority into governed ontology concepts.
  - materiality: This is material because the artifact declares itself the shared concept authority for risk engine and reporting systems. If systems disagree on grade conversion, available limit, or exchange rates, the ontology provides no canonical source or precedence rule to resolve the discrepancy. As a result, central calculations can diverge while still remaining ontology-compliant.
  - action: Add authority-bearing concepts for each shared value class: a canonical RatingScaleMapping with owner, version, and effective period; a LimitAuthority or LimitSnapshot that separates approved_amount from CRM available_limit and records source_system plus precedence; and a governed ExchangeRateSource or ExchangeRate table. The ontology should state which source controls each calculation and how conflicts are resolved before the next stage, because this issue is framed as a next-step blocker that must close before release-quality use.
- issue-012 (high): Collateral is wrongly modeled as a subtype of Exposure. The ontology should treat Collateral as a separate securing-asset entity and connect it to Exposure through the existing secured_by relationship instead of inheritance.
  - root cause: The ontology uses subtype inheritance for Collateral even though its own definitions only support an associative securing relationship to Exposure.
  - materiality: This is material because the ontology is meant to be the shared concept authority for risk engines and reporting systems covering exposures, grades, limits, and collateral. If Collateral inherits Exposure semantics, downstream consumers may count, filter, validate, or report collateral assets as credit exposures, undermining trust in a foundational taxonomy boundary.
  - action: Remove `Collateral.is_a: Exposure`, keep Collateral as an independent securing-asset entity, and retain or clarify the explicit `Exposure secured_by Collateral` association with appropriate role and cardinality. This fixes the type boundary at the source while preserving the valid business relationship between exposures and collateral.
- issue-013 (high): Three credit-grade concepts overlap semantically but the ontology does not define an authoritative internal mapping among them. As a result, Borrower.internal_rating, RiskRating.grade, and Exposure.risk_grade can appear to participate in one shared credit-risk taxonomy while carrying different meanings across engine and reporting consumers.
  - root cause: The ontology defines multiple overlapping rating scales but delegates conversion authority to external wiki/consumer systems instead of making the semantic mapping part of the shared concept model.
  - materiality: This is material because the declared purpose is shared concept authority for ratings used in risk classification and reporting. If the ontology leaves grade conversion and override semantics to each consuming system, it cannot guarantee that the risk engine and reporting outputs refer to the same credit-grade meaning, so classification and reports may disagree while still looking compliant with the taxonomy.
  - action: The required action is to define canonical grade concepts and authority boundaries in the ontology before the next stage. The fix can either model one canonical rating with explicit scale mappings and versioning, or split borrower internal rating, engine borrower rating, and report exposure grade as distinct concepts with governed mapping rules and allowed override semantics. This must happen before release because downstream classification and reporting depend on the same rating meanings being comparable and auditable.
- issue-008 (medium): Stateful credit-risk objects are modeled without lifecycle, terminal, remediation, archival, or post-close correction concepts, so the ontology cannot consistently represent whether exposures, limits, collateral, or risk-appetite compliance records are active, closed, expired, released, remediated, or corrected.
  - root cause: The ontology represents risk objects as current-value records instead of lifecycle-bearing records or event histories.
  - materiality: This weakens the ontology's purpose as shared concepts for risk-engine calculations and reporting outputs because those consumers need common inclusion, exclusion, aging, and correction semantics. Without lifecycle state in the shared taxonomy, one report may treat a closed exposure, expired limit, released collateral item, or remediated breach differently from another even when they are describing the same risk object.
  - action: Add lifecycle/status attributes or lifecycle event concepts for Exposure, Limit, Collateral, and RiskAppetiteCompliance, including effective timestamps, terminal states, and post-period correction or reissue events. The action should establish shared state semantics before downstream risk-engine and reporting rules depend on these objects, because those rules need a common authority for whether a record is current, terminal, remediated, released, or corrected.
- issue-009 (medium): Core product, collateral, borrower segment, and grade classifications are modeled as closed enum literals, so ordinary business classification changes require ontology schema changes instead of governed code-set updates.
  - root cause: Changeable business classification values are modeled as closed enum literals instead of governed, versioned code-set concepts.
  - materiality: This weakens the ontology's extensibility and its role as a shared classification basis for risk engines and reporting systems. When new or retired categories require schema edits and coordinated consumer deployment, classification governance becomes more costly and compatibility risk rises.
  - action: Create versioned code-set entities such as ProductType, CollateralType, BorrowerSegment, and GradeCode with code, label, definition, hierarchy, status, effective_from/effective_to, and source_authority, then have attributes reference those code sets instead of enum literals. This should be handled as follow-up ontology modeling work because the code-set identity and lifecycle model must exist before dependent attributes can safely migrate to references.
- issue-010 (medium): Collateral is incorrectly modeled as an Exposure subtype even though the ontology defines it as a securing asset and already relates Exposure to Collateral through secured_by. This creates a material type-boundary contradiction that should be fixed before the ontology is used as a shared authority.
  - root cause: The ontology uses subtype inheritance (`is_a`) to connect a role/entity that is explicitly defined as a separate secured asset rather than a credit exposure.
  - materiality: The ontology is meant to serve as the shared concept authority for risk engine and reporting systems. If is_a is interpreted as subtype inheritance or class membership, collateral instances become valid exposure instances, so downstream exposure logic, aggregation, validation, or reporting can admit collateral records where only credit exposures should appear.
  - action: Remove Collateral.is_a Exposure and keep Collateral as a separate securing-asset entity related to Exposure through secured_by. If inheritance is needed, introduce or use a more appropriate parent such as RiskMitigant so collateral can share risk-mitigation behavior without becoming an exposure.
- issue-011 (medium): RiskAppetite breach logic is materially defective because it defines breach as ExposureAggregate exceeding RiskAppetite at the entity level, while the ontology only declares ExposureAggregate.total_amount and RiskAppetite.threshold as comparable numeric values.
  - root cause: The risk-appetite breach rule is stated at entity level even though the ontology declares the comparable quantities only as attributes.
  - materiality: This weakens the declared purpose of sharing one risk appetite breach/compliance definition between the risk engine and reporting systems. Consumers implementing the ontology text must infer how to project entity objects into numbers, so the engine and reports may choose different projections or reject the predicate, producing divergent breach status under the same compliance term.
  - action: Rewrite the breach rule as a scoped attribute-level predicate, for example RiskAppetite.compliance_status is breached iff the applicable ExposureAggregate.total_amount is greater than RiskAppetite.threshold. The fix must also define the relation and scope linking the aggregate to the appetite, including borrower segment, portfolio, product, currency, and as_of basis where needed, so all consumers evaluate the same rule.
- issue-014 (medium): LTV is a material ontology issue because it is exposed as a shared ratio-shaped concept without an explicit authority contract. The ontology must decide whether Exposure.ltv is a recomputable derived ratio or a manual review metric, then encode the corresponding formula/provenance, source, currency, and as-of semantics.
  - root cause: A ratio-named field is modeled as an independent manual input while likely source values and valuation timing are also modeled.
  - materiality: This weakens the purpose of providing a shared risk-engine and reporting concept basis for exposure and collateral metrics. If different systems compare or recompute LTV from Exposure.amount and Collateral.appraised_value/appraised_at while the stored value was manually entered under a different basis, risk engines and reports can disagree on collateral adequacy or policy thresholds.
  - action: Close the issue before the next stage by making an ontology-owner decision on the intended meaning of Exposure.ltv. If it is derived, define the authoritative formula, source fields, currency basis, and valuation/as-of timestamp. If it is manual, rename or split it from recomputable LTV and add provenance, review/as-of semantics, and boundary clarity so downstream systems know not to treat it as the same derived ratio.
- issue-015 (medium): Limit utilization is not a stable shared concept because the ontology lets approved limit, available or remaining limit, utilized exposure, and FX conversion basis collapse into the same calculation surface.
  - root cause: The ontology lacks semantic separation and authority rules for approved limit, available limit, utilized exposure, and FX conversion basis.
  - materiality: This weakens shared concept authority between the risk engine and reporting systems. For multi-currency or CRM-integrated borrowers, two systems can both claim to follow the ontology while using different denominator meanings or FX tables, causing limit breach, utilization, and risk-appetite reporting to diverge.
  - action: Separate approved limit, available or remaining limit, and utilized exposure as distinct ontology concepts, then explicitly govern aggregation, currency, as-of date, and FX-source rules. This must be fixed before the next stage because utilization and reporting rules depend on these meanings being stable before risk engine and reporting implementations can rely on them.
- issue-016 (medium): RiskAppetite is a material structural gap: the ontology defines it as the compliance threshold for ExposureAggregate, but the relation graph never connects RiskAppetite to the aggregate or scope it governs.
  - root cause: The ontology declares a RiskAppetite entity and prose comparison rule but does not add any relation involving RiskAppetite in the relations block.
  - materiality: This weakens the ontology's declared role as a shared concept authority for risk engine and reporting systems. If compliance depends on comparing ExposureAggregate to RiskAppetite, consumers need a traversable graph edge that identifies the governed aggregate and scope; prose alone leaves implementations and reports free to infer different borrower segment, portfolio, product, currency, or other scope boundaries.
  - action: Add explicit relation(s) connecting RiskAppetite to the governed ExposureAggregate or to the applicable governing scope, using a relation kind such as governed_by or compares_to. Include required scoping keys, such as borrower segment, portfolio, product, or currency, so risk-engine calculation and reporting interpretation share the same structural contract before the next stage.
- issue-017 (medium): Issue-017 is a material structural lineage gap: Exposure.risk_grade is described as derived from RiskRating.grade, but the ontology graph does not encode that derivation as an explicit relation or typed dependency.
  - root cause: A cross-entity derivation rule was added under classification_rules without a corresponding relation/dependency in the ontology graph.
  - materiality: This weakens the ontology's shared concept basis between risk-engine output and reporting-system classification. Consumers that need the authoritative source path for Exposure.risk_grade can only find the dependency in prose, so different systems may choose different borrower joins, precedence rules, or override behavior while still claiming to follow the ontology.
  - action: Add an explicit relation or typed dependency for the RiskRating.grade to Exposure.risk_grade derivation. The fix should not be a bare edge only: it must also encode or align the authoritative source path, borrower join/scoping rule, and precedence or override behavior with the broader grade-authority semantics before the next stage.
