# Synthesize

## Consensus
- issue-001 (high): OrderLine fulfillment is materially incomplete because the ontology cannot trace each order-line quantity to the shipment or shipment quantity that fulfilled it.
  - root cause: Fulfillment is modeled only at Shipment-to-Order granularity, leaving no line-level allocation path from OrderLine quantities to shipment execution.
  - materiality: The declared purpose is a WMS/TMS/ERP integration baseline for order, inventory, freight, and delivery consistency. Without line-level fulfillment traceability, multi-line orders, partial shipments, split shipments, delayed lines, substitutions, and short picks cannot be reconciled between OMS promises, WMS picking and packing, TMS shipment execution, and delivery evidence.
  - action: Add a line-level fulfillment/allocation concept, such as FulfillmentLine or ShipmentLine, linking order_line_ref, shipment_ref, shipped_qty, picked_qty, fulfilled_at, and status. Keep the order-level Shipment-to-Order relation only as a derived summary if needed, because the line-level allocation must be the authoritative path for reconciliation.
- issue-002 (high): Inventory quantities are modeled as current or nightly aggregate numbers without enough temporal, movement, reservation, lifecycle, and derivation structure. As a result, the ontology cannot reliably audit allocation decisions or evolve from nightly batch inventory sync to event-based synchronization while preserving the meaning of existing quantity fields.
  - root cause: Inventory is represented as current or nightly aggregate quantities rather than time-qualified snapshots, movements, reservations, or effective intervals.
  - materiality: This is material because the declared purpose depends on inventory consistency, allocation correctness, and stable WMS/ERP inventory authority across fulfillment operations. If inventory changes between nightly snapshots or orders are allocated intraday, the model cannot reconstruct the inventory basis for an accepted order, explain oversell or undersell disputes, or reconcile WMS physical stock with ERP accounting stock over time.
  - action: Fix this before relying on the model for allocation or release-quality inventory integration. Introduce InventorySnapshot, InventoryMovement or InventoryTransaction, and Reservation or Allocation concepts with as_of or effective time, source_system, lifecycle status, quantity deltas, and explicit derivation rules for available_qty. The aggregate-to-record provenance concern should remain aligned with the related issue-014 context, but it does not replace the broader temporal and lifecycle repair required here.
- issue-006 (high): DeliveryLeg must not remain a subtype of Shipment. The current inheritance makes a route segment carry the meaning and state contract of an end-to-end shipment, so the ontology should treat legs as shipment components with their own execution state model.
  - root cause: DeliveryLeg is modeled as a subtype of Shipment even though it is semantically a route segment or component of an end-to-end shipment.
  - materiality: This weakens the ontology's purpose as a WMS/TMS/ERP integration baseline because Shipment is a central integration concept whose meaning and status enum need to stay stable over time. If leg, handoff, return, customs, or multimodal states are forced through Shipment.status, future domain additions become integration-wide reinterpretations rather than local extensions, and whole-shipment tracking semantics can be confused with route-leg semantics.
  - action: Remove the DeliveryLeg is_a Shipment inheritance and model DeliveryLeg, or a TransportMovement equivalent, as a component of Shipment through has_leg or part_of. Keep Shipment as the order-fulfillment cargo movement, give DeliveryLeg its own status/ETA/carrier fields where needed, and support future customs, handoff, return, and route-event states through leg/event state extensions or mappings before broadening the shipment domain.
- issue-007 (high): Carrier status changes and new carrier onboarding cannot preserve a shared interpretation of shipment status because the ontology delegates carrier-event to internal-status mapping to individual integrations instead of owning that mapping as a shared concept.
  - root cause: External carrier events and internal Shipment status mappings are delegated to individual integrations rather than modeled as ontology-owned mapping authority.
  - materiality: This weakens the declared purpose of providing a common freight and delivery status interpretation across TMS and carrier integrations. When carrier codes change, a new carrier is onboarded, or exception events are subdivided, each integration can decide the mapping separately, so the same external carrier event may become different Shipment statuses and historical status meaning cannot be preserved consistently.
  - action: Separate carrier source event codes from internal standard shipment states, then add a versioned TrackingStatusMapping or status-transition rule concept as ontology authority. New carriers and carrier code changes should be absorbed by adding or versioning mapping records, while changes to the internal Shipment.status enum remain minimized and controlled.
- issue-009 (high): InventoryRecord.quantity_on_hand is a material authority-collapse issue because it presents WMS physical stock and ERP accounting stock as one current on-hand quantity.
  - root cause: InventoryRecord.quantity_on_hand collapses WMS physical stock and ERP accounting stock into one attribute without a source-authority distinction.
  - materiality: The ontology is meant to define consistent inventory authority and meaning across WMS/TMS/ERP integration. When WMS physical inventory and ERP accounting inventory can disagree, a single quantity_on_hand value lets integrators mistake order promise, picking availability, accounting adjustment, and reconciliation quantities for the same authoritative fact, weakening the baseline's operational and governance purpose.
  - action: Fix this before release by splitting inventory quantities by authority and basis, for example physical_on_hand_qty for WMS-authoritative warehouse stock and accounting_on_hand_qty for ERP-authoritative accounting stock. Add reconciliation or adjustment-event concepts, including relevant basis or timing, so differences between the two quantities are modeled explicitly instead of hidden behind one field.
- issue-010 (high): InventoryAggregate.available_qty is a material high-severity temporal/semantic defect: it exposes a nightly inventory snapshot as unqualified current availability and allocation rules use it for intraday allocation as if it were current allocatable stock.
  - root cause: A nightly snapshot value is exposed as unqualified current availability and used for intraday allocation without an explicit time basis.
  - materiality: This weakens the integration baseline's purpose of making order and inventory temporal meaning and operational risk explicit. When intraday orders, reservations, or shipments change stock, a prior nightly snapshot can still be read as current availability, making availability ambiguous between past snapshot quantity and current allocatable quantity and creating oversell, under-allocation, and cross-system inventory drift risk.
  - action: Fix the model before release by renaming or remodeling available_qty as a time-qualified snapshot, including as_of_at and the reservation or allocation basis used to compute it. Intraday allocation should depend on a separate real-time ATP or reservation-ledger authority so current allocation decisions are not made from a prior nightly snapshot.
- issue-004 (medium): The ontology materially lacks source-authority and override/audit evidence for operational values that are reconciled across systems or manually changed, especially inventory quantity and shipment ETA.
  - root cause: Shared and manually adjusted operational values are represented only as final scalar attributes, without source-authority or adjustment-event concepts.
  - materiality: This weakens authority, auditability, and operational risk control for WMS/TMS/ERP integration because downstream users cannot tell which system owns a disputed value, whether a human changed it, why it changed, or which value should be trusted. That leaves dispute resolution, SLA explanations, and reconciliation audit trails outside the model.
  - action: Add SourceAuthority or SourceSystem ownership for shared operational values and add an Adjustment or OverrideEvent concept covering actor_ref, occurred_at, reason_code, source_value, adjusted_value, approved_by, and affected field. Apply this at minimum to ETA overrides and inventory reconciliation so authority and audit evidence are modeled before the ontology proceeds as an integration governance baseline.
- issue-005 (medium): Order, Shipment, and Tracking lifecycle coverage is materially incomplete because it models the normal success path but not common exception, reverse-logistics, and post-terminal fulfillment paths.
  - root cause: Lifecycle coverage is success-path oriented and leaves exception, post-delivery, and reverse-logistics states outside the core Order and Shipment status models.
  - materiality: This weakens status model completeness for order, shipment, and delivery operations: failed delivery, cancellation, backorder, return/RMA, loss, damage, carrier exception, and post-delivery correction cannot be represented as first-class lifecycle outcomes. As a result, OMS, TMS, and carrier streams must reconcile these cases through integration-specific mappings rather than through the ontology baseline.
  - action: Extend lifecycle coverage before the ontology advances as a fulfillment operations baseline. Add explicit terminal and exception states and lifecycle event concepts for cancellation, backorder, return/RMA, delivery failure, loss/damage, correction/reissue, and closure, and define how carrier TrackingEvent.exception drives shipment and order lifecycle transitions.
- issue-008 (medium): DeliveryLeg inherits Shipment.status.delivered as its final state, so an intermediate route leg is modeled as if completing that leg were the same terminal event as delivering the whole shipment to the customer. This is a retained medium issue, scoped as a downstream manifestation of the broader DeliveryLeg-as-Shipment modeling problem.
  - root cause: Route segments inherit Shipment's unqualified final delivery state because DeliveryLeg is declared as a Shipment subtype.
  - materiality: The declared purpose is a WMS/TMS/ERP integration concept baseline with consistent shipment and delivery status semantics. The inherited final state weakens that purpose because an integration handling a multi-leg route can no longer tell whether delivered means a warehouse-to-hub leg is complete or the customer shipment is complete. That ambiguity can cause downstream systems to close the whole shipment too early, or avoid closing a leg because doing so appears to imply customer delivery.
  - action: Fix the DeliveryLeg redesign before relying on status semantics in integrations. Either remove DeliveryLeg from Shipment inheritance and model it as a segment/composition of a shipment, or split the status model so Shipment.status and DeliveryLeg.status have distinct terminal meanings, such as delivered_to_customer for shipments and arrived_destination for legs. The ordering matters because the final-state vocabulary cannot be made reliable while the parent inheritance still imposes Shipment's unqualified delivered state on every leg.
  - unresolved disagreement: Evolution accepts the defect as real but prefers folding it under the broader issue-006 DeliveryLeg-as-Shipment root; deliberation narrowed issue-008 to a distinct downstream final-state over-constraint rather than an independent top-level root.
- issue-011 (medium): The ontology must not treat the shared label `delivered` as a single semantic state across order fulfillment, shipment status, and carrier tracking events. Each use has a different authority and completion condition, so the current model leaves delivery completion meaning ambiguous.
  - root cause: Identical status labels across OMS, TMS, and carrier events are not semantically namespaced or mapped under a shared authority.
  - materiality: This weakens the declared purpose of semantic consistency and precedence across order, shipment, and delivery status models. If OMS, TMS, or carrier `delivered` values are treated as equivalent, integrations can trigger order completion, customer notification, settlement, or SLA decisions from the wrong authority or at the wrong completion point.
  - action: Namespace or separate the status concepts by authority and completion condition, such as carrier delivered event, shipment delivery completed, and order fulfillment delivered. Then define standard transition and mapping rules among carrier events, shipment completion, and order fulfillment completion before downstream integrations rely on status equivalence.
- issue-012 (medium): Shipment.total_weight is a material medium issue because it is a bare numeric derived total even though SKU weights and dimensions may arrive in different source units. It should be retained as a distinct shipment aggregate correctness issue, while recognizing it as a narrower manifestation of the broader measurement-unit problem in issue-003.
  - root cause: Shipment.total_weight is a derived aggregate over SKU/order-line weights without preserving unit or conversion basis.
  - materiality: The declared purpose is to provide consistent freight and inventory measurement semantics for WMS/TMS/ERP integration. That purpose is weakened when a shipment total can combine kg and lb source weights without preserving the unit or conversion basis, because the resulting number may no longer mean a real total weight and can mislead freight rating, load-limit checks, and carrier integration decisions.
  - action: Model weight and dimensions as value-plus-unit measurements, and store Shipment.total_weight with its unit, conversion basis, calculation time, and source or derivation context. This should be handled alongside the broader measurement modeling work for issue-003, because fixing the shipment total depends on a shared representation for compatible units and conversions.
- issue-013 (medium): OrderLine lacks a line-granularity fulfillment connection to Shipment, so the ontology only represents fulfillment at Shipment-to-Order level. This remains a material medium structural completeness issue under the broader line-fulfillment gap.
  - root cause: The fulfillment relation is modeled only as Shipment-to-Order while OrderLine has no incoming or outgoing fulfillment relation.
  - materiality: The declared purpose is a WMS/TMS/ERP integration concept baseline across order, inventory allocation, shipment, and delivery. Without a connected path from each OrderLine quantity to the Shipment or DeliveryLeg that fulfilled it, the model cannot represent partial shipments, split fulfillment, or multi-leg delivery reconciliation inside the ontology; downstream systems must supply out-of-model joins or assumptions.
  - action: Add a line-level fulfillment concept such as ShipmentLine or FulfillmentAllocation that references both OrderLine and Shipment and carries fulfilled quantity, timing, and status fields as needed. This should be handled as the structural connectivity work item under the broader fulfillment-allocation fix so that order lines, shipment execution, and delivery evidence reconcile through explicit ontology paths.
- issue-014 (medium): InventoryAggregate is a material structural provenance gap: it is used as the SKU-level allocation availability concept, but the ontology does not connect it to the warehouse-level InventoryRecord records that would justify, execute, or reconcile that availability.
  - root cause: InventoryAggregate is modeled as a standalone SKU-level entity with no declared aggregate/source relation to InventoryRecord.
  - materiality: This weakens the declared purpose of a concept baseline for inventory allocation in WMS/TMS/ERP integration because allocation decisions must often be traced from enterprise available_qty back to warehouse stock records for picking and reconciliation. Without that path, the value used to accept orders is disconnected from execution and audit evidence, reducing actionability and auditability.
  - action: Fix this before the next stage by adding an explicit aggregates or derived-from relation from InventoryAggregate to InventoryRecord, or by modeling an InventorySnapshot/AvailabilityByWarehouse concept with source InventoryRecord references, aggregation time, and scope. If the enterprise aggregate is intentionally independent, its authoritative source relation still needs to be documented explicitly so allocation, reconciliation, and audit paths have a declared basis.
- issue-015 (medium): DeliveryLeg is structurally under-modeled as a route segment: it has sequence and endpoint fields, but no parent Shipment or route composition path that says which end-to-end shipment it belongs to.
  - root cause: DeliveryLeg is modeled through inheritance but lacks a composition relation from Shipment to DeliveryLeg.
  - materiality: This weakens the declared purpose of shipment and delivery path integration because multi-leg shipment execution depends on assembling segment records into one shipment movement. Without a parent route or shipment scope, leg_seq cannot reliably order legs for a specific shipment, and the ontology cannot determine which DeliveryLeg instances compose the same route.
  - action: Model DeliveryLeg as a component of Shipment by adding a required parent shipment_ref or an explicit Shipment has_leg DeliveryLeg relation, and scope leg_seq to that parent shipment. If the subtype modeling is retained for compatibility, add an explicit parent_shipment_ref so route composition is still represented.
  - unresolved disagreement: No material disagreement remains. Structure accepted the issue directly, supporting lenses kept the medium-severity route-completeness impact, and axiology's insufficient evidence was not treated as a conflict because it lacked value-authority anchors rather than disputing the structural claim.
